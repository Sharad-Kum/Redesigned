Organizational Design as a Leadership Capability

As execution accelerates across AI-enabled platforms and connected systems, the limiting factor in most organizations is no longer the speed of the technology — it is how teams are structured and whether incentives are aligned across sales, solution, delivery, and market impact.

Structure is not a background condition. In an accelerating environment, it is either the enabler or the bottleneck.

SK Telecom · South Korea
Restructured its businesses twice — into four units in 2024 and seven in 2025
Sharpening ownership and execution capability at each layer of its AI strategy
Unilever · United Kingdom
Replaced a global matrix structure with five outcome-accountable business groups
Result: underlying operating profit up 12.6%, gross margin at its highest in a decade
Nestlé · Switzerland
Introduced an “Operational Master Plan,” replacing Functional Objectives with Group Objectives
CHF 2.5 billion in targeted savings tied directly to the structural reset
Structures Optimized For
Functional depth, local metrics, sequential handoffs, stable hierarchy.
→
Leading Organizations Redesign Toward
Solution ownership, shared outcomes, cross-functional coherence, continuous redesign.

One consistent pattern — organizational design is not a one-time decision. It is an ongoing leadership capability. Organizations that redesign proactively are the ones remaining ahead on the maturity curve.
