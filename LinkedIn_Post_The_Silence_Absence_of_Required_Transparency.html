The Silence, Absence of Required Transparency: Closure

Twelve posts — and the clearest finding wasn't about content. It was about what stays undisclosed by design.

Transparency is never about exposing the algorithm. It's about giving creators enough to draw the line for themselves. Right now, that line isn't drawn for us.

Distribution
Evolved early in each post’s lifecycle, then flattened sharply within hours
The Within:Outside split moved from roughly 80:20 in the first minutes to 92:8 within hours
The impressions-to-reach ratio held in a narrow band (6.28–9.07, averaging 7.66) through the first 15 hours
Engagement
Impressions and reach did not move together the way conventional wisdom assumes
Between hour 1 and hour 15, impressions rose 56% — reach rose 129%
Content Presentation & Platform Observability
Text-only versus text + image produced no conclusive distribution or engagement advantage either way
Manual analytics refreshes mostly coincided with shifts in reported impressions — reporting lag, or the act of checking itself, impossible to say
Some analytics stayed unavailable on certain posts, suggesting reporting views unlock only past undisclosed thresholds

What surfaced wasn't a playbook. It was a set of anomalies the available data couldn't resolve — and shouldn't have to remain unresolved. How do recommendation logic, subscription tier, company affiliation, and reporting thresholds actually shape distribution?

If you've run your own experiments on distribution, recommendation, and observability platforms — what have you had to guess at that you shouldn't have?
