Seats Still Exist

A title can merge or split. Questions still need an owner.

Edition 6 placed AI inside the H. This one closes the arc. Across GTM, Marketing, Sales and the H, who owns the questions?

Titles are the first thing to move.

Three companies, three directions
Kohl’s retired its chief marketing officer title in August. Marketing, brand, loyalty, media and digital commerce now sit under a new chief customer officer.
Six Flags, the U.S. theme park operator, went the other way in May. It split its chief commercial officer role into a CMO and an SVP, Commercial.
Coca-Cola retired its global CMO in 2017 for a chief growth officer. In late 2019 it brought the CMO back, with strategy moving to the CFO and commercial operations to the COO.

Three companies, three directions. The questions never left. Someone still owns who the customer is, what is on offer, and how it reaches them.

That is a seat. Not a title, not a reporting line. The place where every question has an owner.

A question dropped or answered twice shows up later as lag, not conflict. That is the signal to relook the seats.

AI accelerates data points for answers. It does not own the answers. A model can draft the segment, the price and the plan. It cannot be held to them.

Structures will keep shifting. The questions won't. Wherever they land, that is the seat.

When answers arrive faster than a seat can review them, who is still held to them?
